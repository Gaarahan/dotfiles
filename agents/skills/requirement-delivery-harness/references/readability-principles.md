# Review Readability Principles

- Lead with conclusions. Do not encode confirmed decisions as questions and answers.
- Keep one subject per paragraph or list block. Split dense paragraphs at changes in actor, responsibility, condition, or outcome.
- Remove implementation detail that does not affect a reviewer decision, contract, risk, or acceptance criterion.
- Turn long unformatted sequences into titled lists. Organize multi-module responsibilities by role or module.
- Explain a new internal concept at first mention, including its owner and purpose.
- Use Mermaid for architecture, branching flows, and sequences when prose would force the reader to reconstruct relationships.
- In sequence diagrams, show activation where useful. Use green for new behavior, yellow for modified behavior, and no fill for reused behavior; include a legend.
- Prefer direct final-state sentences over contrastive or historical wording.
